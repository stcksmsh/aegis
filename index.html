Aegis copies your important folders to a USB drive and locks them with a passphrase only you know. If your laptop dies, gets stolen or gets hit by ransomware, plug the drive into any computer and get your files back. If someone steals the drive, they get nothing.

    How it works
    
        
      
          
    1. Pick folders. Documents, Pictures, Desktop, or anything else.
      2. 
          
    2. Pick a passphrase. Write it down. No one, not even Aegis, can recover it.
      3. 
          
    3. Plug in a USB drive. Aegis sets it up without deleting what's already on it.
      4. 
          
    4. Done. Next time you plug the drive in, Aegis backs up by itself. Only changed files are copied, so later backups are fast.
      5. 
        
    
        
    Restore: open Aegis → Restore → pick a date → pick a folder. New computer? Install Aegis, plug in the drive, type your passphrase.
    
        
        
    
          
          
        
    
      
    

      
    
        
    What you get
    
        
      
          
    • Strong encryptionEvery backup is encrypted (AES-256) before it touches the drive.
      • 
          
    • AutomaticBacks up when you plug in your drive. Runs quietly in the tray or menu bar.
      • 
          
    • HistoryKeeps every past version until you choose to clean up. Get back last week's file.
      • 
          
    • Get back one fileBrowse any past backup and restore just the files you need. Never overwrites what's already there.
      • 
          
    • RemindersNudges you when it's been a week since your last backup. Optional hourly backups while the drive stays plugged in.
      • 
          
    • Drive spaceShows free space and warns before your drive fills up.
      • 
          
    • Safe to unplugUnplugging mid-backup never damages earlier backups.
      • 
          
    • Checks itselfVerifies each backup after it finishes.
      • 
          
    • No lock-inBackups use the open restic format. Readable without Aegis.
      • 
          
    • PrivateNo accounts, no cloud, no internet. Your passphrase is never saved to disk (optionally kept in your system keychain).
      • 
          
    • Every desktopSame app on Windows, macOS and Linux. A drive made on one works on the others (use exFAT).
      • 
        
    
      
    

      
    
        
    Lose the drive, not the files
    
        
    
          
    
            
            
            
            
          
    
          
    
            
    On the drive
    
            
    
            
            
            
    
          
    
          
    This runs your browser's Web Crypto (AES-256-GCM, key from PBKDF2) as an illustration. Aegis adds no crypto of its own: backups are restic repositories (AES-256-CTR + Poly1305, scrypt key derivation).
    
        
    
        
      
          
    • Passphrase never written to disk or logs, never passed on a command line. Optional storage in the OS keychain; paranoid mode turns that off.
      • 
          
    • Local API listens on 127.0.0.1 only and accepts requests only from the Aegis window, which blocks websites from talking to it.
      • 
          
    • Drives are untrusted. Drive markers hold no secrets and are sanitized when read.
      • 
          
    • Restore never overwrites existing files.
      • 
          
    • Supply chain: the bundled restic is a pinned release, checked against restic's published SHA-256 checksums at build time.
      • 
          
    • Out of scope: malware already running as you, or someone who forces you to reveal your passphrase.
      •
